# Onboarding Copy

## Tone

Write like a calm product designer, not a performance marketer.
Use short sentences. Prefer concrete language over slogans.

## Headline rules

- Lead with the user's outcome, not the app's brand line.
- Keep it short enough to scan in one glance.
- Make one promise at a time.
- Prefer specificity over intensity.

Better:
- `Build your first weekly plan`
- `See where your money went`
- `Find workouts that fit your schedule`

Worse:
- `Transform your life today`
- `Unlock your full potential`
- `The smartest way to level up`

## Question rules

- Ask one thing at a time.
- Use words the user would actually say.
- Offer 3-6 options unless the domain truly needs more.
- Make every answer change the next step, result, or recommendation.

Better:
- `What are you trying to get out of this app?`
- `What usually gets in the way?`

Worse:
- `Tell us about your journey`
- `Which of these best describes your optimization challenges?`

## CTA rules

Make the CTA describe the next action.

Better:
- `Build my first plan`
- `See my recommendations`
- `Turn on reminders`
- `Save this result`

Worse:
- `Continue`
- `Next`
- `Submit`

## Social proof rules

Use only proof the team can stand behind.
If proof is unavailable, omit it or mark it as placeholder content during planning.
Do not invent user counts, ratings, or outcome stats.

## Screen-by-screen guidance

### Welcome
State the benefit in one line.
Support it with one visual that matches the real app.

### Goal selection
Use options that sound mutually distinct.
Let the user recognize themselves quickly.

### Friction or preference selection
Use specific blockers or constraints.
Avoid diagnostic language.

### Personalized solution
Mirror back what the user said.
Explain what the app will do next, not everything it can do.

### Permission priming
Describe the benefit, not the operating-system concept.

### Paywall
Restate the value in plain language.
Show plan details clearly.
Avoid fake urgency and manipulative button copy.

## Final pass

Before finalizing copy, check:
- Would a real user say this out loud?
- Does each screen earn its place?
- Does the copy sound credible for this specific product?
- Does the flow help the user start using the app faster?
