# CTR Optimization Workflow

Use when Search Console shows pages/queries with strong impressions and decent positions but weak click-through rate.

## Goal

Improve clicks without changing rankings.

## Step 1: Find candidates

Best targets usually have:
- high impressions
- average position between **1 and 10**
- CTR below what you'd expect for that position

Common candidate types:
- blog posts with bland titles
- category pages with generic SERP copy
- comparison pages without a clear angle
- feature pages where the title does not match query intent

## Step 2: Inspect the current SERP promise

Check:
- current title
- current meta description
- query intent
- whether the title matches what searchers want
- whether competitors use stronger hooks

## Step 3: Rewrite carefully

Good title improvements usually add one of:
- clearer intent match
- specific outcome
- audience qualifier
- freshness/year where truly relevant
- comparison framing

Bad rewrites usually add:
- clickbait
- keyword stuffing
- fake urgency
- exaggerated claims

## Strong title patterns

- `How to [Outcome] in 2026`
- `[A] vs [B]: Which Is Better for [Audience]?`
- `Best [Category] for [Audience]`
- `[Tool/Feature] Pricing, Features, and Best Fit`

## Meta description rules

- support the title, do not repeat it verbatim
- clarify what the page includes
- include one concrete detail if possible
- keep it natural, not stuffed

## Step 4: Change one thing at a time on important pages

For major pages:
- avoid changing title, meta, H1, and URL all at once
- keep the page intent stable
- log the date of the change

## Step 5: Recheck after 2-4 weeks

Because GSC lags, do not evaluate after 2 days.

Review:
- CTR change
- impressions stability
- position stability
- whether Google rewrote your title/description anyway

## Common CTR issues to look for

- title starts with brand instead of topic
- meta description is missing or generic
- wrong page ranking for the query intent
- title too broad while query is specific
- no freshness signal on time-sensitive content
- title truncated in SERP

## When not to optimize CTR first

Do not start here if:
- the page has indexation problems
- rankings are poor because the page intent is wrong
- the page content is weak/thin
- the page is cannibalized by another URL

CTR work is for pages already getting seen.
